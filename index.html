    Eight battles across the rival homelands. Your chosen army fights them all.

    Rules of war
    • Drag from your castle to any castle to send troops. Keys 1–4 or the scroll wheel choose how many: 25%, 50%, 75% or all.
    • Drag across several of your castles to attack from all of them. Or tap castles to select, then tap a target.
    • Special power: press Q or click the power panel in the top left of the map. The panel shows what your power does and counts down until it's ready: 45 seconds into the battle, then every five minutes.
    • Coins: every castle you hold earns coins each minute: small 1, medium 2, large 3. Save up to buy one map unit per battle (B opens the shop): a Ballista Tower (40), Siege Trebuchet (65) or Great Ward (90), placed near your castles.
    • Upgrades: select one of your castles to spend its troops on Walls (defenders count more, archers shoot faster and farther) or Barracks (faster training, stronger soldiers), three levels each. U and I upgrade every selected castle. A captured castle loses one level of each.
    • Archers on every castle shoot at enemy columns marching past.
    • Special castles carry a badge beside their troop count. A fortress (shield) counts every defender double. A war camp (crossed swords) trains 60% faster but has weak walls. A village (cottage) trains nothing, but your castles within its dashed circle train 25% faster.
    • Armies that meet on the road fight. The stronger column marches on with what's left.
    • Red banners over your castles count the enemy troops marching on them. A pulsing ring means the castle will fall unless you reinforce it.
    • Rivals surrender when they're down to a tenth of the troops on the map and two castles or fewer. Against one rival, their castles open their gates to you. In the Grand Campaign a realm gives up once it has fallen far behind the strongest, from wave 20 on.
    • Rally points: right-drag (or Shift-drag, or long-press then drag on a touchscreen) from one of your castles to another. New troops then march there automatically, leaving 5 at home. Right-click the castle, or drag and release on empty ground, to clear it.
    • Saving: an unfinished battle is saved in this browser when you close or hide the tab, and every 10 seconds. Resume it from the top of this menu. Retreating to the menu abandons it. The Grand Campaign has its own five save slots (see below).
    • Upkeep and the garrison cap: a castle trains at half speed once its garrison passes twice its size, and a quarter speed past four times; an hourglass on its plaque shows when. It also holds at most 60, 90 or 120 troops by size, plus 15 per Walls level (the Garrison cap setting scales this): troops above the cap desert, about a third of the excess a minute. Spread big armies out or put them to work.
    • Rivers can only be crossed at bridges, so bridges become chokepoints. Forests slow marching troops. While aiming, the dashed line shows the route each column will take.
    • Troop types (the Troops buttons, or T to switch): Foot are all-rounders. Cavalry march 1.6× as fast but hit castles at only 0.6×, so use them to rush reinforcements and catch columns. Catapults crawl at 0.55× and fight at 0.75× on the road, but hit castles 1.8× as hard; send foot alongside to protect them.
    • Truces (three or more kingdoms): use Diplomacy to offer a rival lord a 90-second truce, or accept one from the offer card that appears when a lord proposes one (it waits 15 seconds). Allies can't attack each other, their columns pass on the road, and their castles fly a white pennant. Isolde and Amaru offer truces when they're second; Amaru wants 15 coins; Torvek never agrees; and Veyra always says yes, then breaks her word within a minute or so. You can break a truce in Diplomacy too: the lord you betray takes it badly, and neither of you can agree another for a minute.
    • Weather drifts across each homeland: rain on the vale, dust storms on the steppe, snow in the fjords, heat (and dust) in the desert, marsh fog (and rain) in the mire. It slows marching, training or sight for a minute or so, except for the army whose homeland it is. Night falls every four minutes and trains troops up to 10% slower.
    • Fog of war (optional): you only see near your own castles and marching columns. Castles out of sight show what you last saw, greyed. Send troops out to scout. The rival lords play by the same rules.
    • Free-for-all pits you against all four other armies at once on a bigger map. The rival lords fight each other too.
    • Map events (optional, under Lord taunts): every minute or two something happens, announced with a banner. Mercenaries fill an unclaimed keep with 30 troops who join whoever takes it (gold ring). Plague takes 30% from a random held castle. Bandits send a grey raiding column at the weakest castle; a castle they overrun is left unclaimed. A harvest makes one kingdom's castles train half again as fast for 30 seconds. In the Grand Campaign one opens every fourth march.
    • Two players on one screen (the toggle under Fog of war): a second person takes the rival army at the keyboard with W A S D, E (select or send), F (select all), R (power), Shift+1–4 (send amount), T (offer or break a truce) and Y / N (answer one). No lord plays that kingdom, fog is off, and the end screen names the winner.
    • Grand Campaign: all five armies on one great map, played in waves. In the plan phase nothing moves: drag orders as usual, change or remove them in the orders panel, then press March! (or Enter) and every army's orders play out for 20 seconds before the next plan. Pan with WASD or by dragging empty ground, zoom with the wheel or a pinch, and watch the minimap. Pause to save to one of five slots or export a save file; the campaign is also autosaved every wave and can be continued, loaded or deleted from the menu. Each map has a monster of its own.
    • King of the Hill (under Modes): a crowned keep stands at the centre of the map. It is big, its walls count 1.5×, and it can't be upgraded. Whoever holds it scores a point a second, shown on the score bar in the header; the first to 300 wins, and after 12 minutes the most points wins. Each kingdom's starting castle (gold pennant) can be emptied but never taken. A banner calls out the leader, and at 250 points every lord turns on them. The lords know the rule: Torvek rushes the hill, Isolde takes the keeps beside it and strikes the moment someone else has paid to take it, Amaru builds a Ballista Tower next to it, Veyra waits for the holder to bleed, and Sigrun freezes the holder's columns with Winter's Grip. Your best time to 300 is kept for each army.
    • Story campaigns: each army's five chapters set their own objective, shown on a badge at the bottom of the map: take every enemy castle, hold out until the timer runs down, or take the castle ringed in gold. Some chapters start you outnumbered or in a single fortress. Progress is kept per army.
    • Map monsters roam the Grand Campaign maps and bite any column that strays within reach. Drag troops onto one to fight it: whoever lands the final blow takes the whole coin bounty, and its health bar shows every army's share of the damage. The lords ignore a healthy monster and swarm it once it is nearly dead.
    • Bigger castles train troops faster. Unclaimed keeps never grow. A captured castle is rebuilt in its new owner's style.
    • Space selects all your castles. [ and ] change the game speed (1×, 1.5× or 2×). P pauses. M mutes sound effects and N the music.
